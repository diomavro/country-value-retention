# country-value-retention

**Who captures the value generated inside a country?**

This is an open, auditable accounting system. For every euro of value added
produced in a country, it records who ultimately receives it: resident workers,
the state, resident owners, or foreign workers, owners, lenders and EU
institutions. It also breaks the result down by industry and by recipient
country. Cyprus is the first country implemented. The design is
country-generic, so the rest of the EU can be added from the same Eurostat
sources.

> **Cyprus, headline (2023, central estimate).** The official national
> accounts (rest-of-world account) record primary income paid abroad equal to
> **108% of GDP**. Almost
> all of that is income passing through Cypriot holding companies. Income
> *generated by production in Cyprus* that accrues abroad (profits of
> foreign-owned firms, interest to foreign lenders and parents, pay to
> non-resident workers, EU duties) is about **7.6% of GDP**, so at most
> **~92%** of the value produced accrues to residents. Given the central
> assumptions the figure is an upper bound: outflows the statistics miss count
> as retained. A sector-by-sector bridge (`data/processed/bridge.parquet`)
> accounts for the difference between the two numbers. See
> [`paper/`](paper/) and [`docs/limitations.md`](docs/limitations.md). The
> authoritative figures are those in `data/processed`; this summary is updated
> by hand.

## The idea in four sentences

1. **Revenue is not value.** Only value added (revenue minus purchased inputs) counts, so a €20 platform fee is never "€20 of leakage".
2. **Only the owners' share of profit follows ownership abroad.** Profit means after depreciation, interest and Cypriot corporate tax. Wages paid to residents and taxes paid in Cyprus stay, whoever owns the firm.
3. **Residence, not nationality.** "Foreign" means non-resident, as in the national accounts.
4. **Imports and value added are two slices of the same euro.** The input-output model splits every euro of spending exactly once.

Read [`docs/concepts.md`](docs/concepts.md) for these ideas worked through with
examples, then [`docs/methodology.md`](docs/methodology.md) for the precise
rules.

## Reproduce

Requires Python 3.12, [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/), a TeX distribution
(for the paper) and Node ≥ 20 (for the website).

```bash
uv sync
make ingest      # download official sources (~40 MB)
make process     # parse raw files
make model       # Frame A/B, IO indicators, ownership, sensitivity -> data/processed, data/cvr.duckdb
make validate    # unit tests + accounting identities; non-zero exit on failure
make paper       # figures, generated numbers and tables, compile paper/main.pdf
make dashboard   # export JSON and build the static site in website/out
```

`make` unsets `PYTHONPATH` so the locked environment is the only package
source.

## Data sources

The sources are official statistics: CYSTAT supply-use and input-output tables
(2010–2022) and sector accounts; Eurostat national accounts, FATS
(foreign-controlled enterprises), balance of payments (by sector and partner,
FDI income with SPE split) and FIGARO; the Central Bank of Cyprus (SPE
definition, cross-checks). Company ownership comes from audited annual reports
and regulatory filings. Every row carries a source tier and a URL. See
`data_catalogue.csv`.

## Limitations (short version)

- The non-resident equity share θ (calibrated on company filings) and the corporate tax rate are parameters. Results are shown across their ranges, including variants that test whether retention is overstated.
- Retention is an upper bound: outflows the statistics miss count as retained.
- 22–26% of FATS cells are confidential. Unpublished recipients are reported as such, never guessed.
- SPEs are removed by a sector rule, not entity by entity.
- No causal claims: the accounting says where income goes, not whether foreign ownership helped or hurt.

Full list: [`docs/limitations.md`](docs/limitations.md).
